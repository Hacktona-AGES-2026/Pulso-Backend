Summary

Everything we have decided so far, in one place. The same content, split by domain, plus the code and the dataset, lives in the ideas/ folder.

Today the surgical queue is ordered by entry date plus a priority someone typed once, so a patient who gets worse never moves up. Pulso rebuilds the order from the patient's condition: clinical events, waiting time and an estimated risk. Every position comes with its reasons, and every tier change is confirmed by a physician. Around that engine, Pulso is a platform for the whole hospital, where each role adds signals and gets its own to-do list.

Pitch

  • Conecta hospitais, pacientes e cirurgiões
  • Prioriza por condição clínica, não por data de entrada
  • Se atualiza a partir de eventos clínicos, tempo de espera e risco
  • Explica cada posição na fila
  • Valida toda mudança com um médico responsável
  • Se adapta a equipe, recursos e complexidade de cada hospital
  • Combina paciente, cirurgia, sala e equipe
  • Usa IA (machine learning + agentes) para apoiar a decisão médica, sem substituí-la

The challenge

Desafio 1: Priorização Inteligente da Fila Cirúrgica.

Sponsor. Fin-x is a B2B health-tech company serving hospitals, private healthcare and SUS. They process more than 100k surgery orders a month across the whole surgical journey: pedido, autorização, agenda, pré-operatório, cirurgia, remuneração. They use AI heavily, with models and agents for specialized analysis, rule validation, insights, predictability and efficiency. They are remote-first.

Problem. Citing TCU numbers on surgical queues: “Hoje a fila mede o tempo, e não a pessoa.” The queue is sorted by entry date and a manual priority set on the day of inclusion, which makes it hard to manage the most critical patients.

Fixed

  • Focus on prioritization: who goes first and why.
  • The base is the dataset, the whole queue.
  • Every decision must be explainable.
  • The solution outputs an order for the queue.

Free

  • Score engine, decision support, agent, dashboard, process.
  • Method: rules, weights, SWALIS, statistics, ML, LLM, consensus.

Golden rule. No patient with a malignant neoplasm (CID starting with C) may sit below a patient with no clinical urgency. Use the CID, not the flag.

Deliverables

  • fila_priorizada.csv with solicitacao_id, posicao, justificativa: one row per active request.
  • DECISOES.md explaining what was left out and why.
  • Demo and pitch. This is what the jury evaluates.
  • Self-check: python verificar_fila_priorizada.py fila_priorizada.csv

Product: Pulso for the whole hospital

The algorithm is one part. The product is the whole hospital working from the same living queue.

The queue is built from events

Pulso stores events: request created, pre-op exam done, ER visit, patient reported more pain, authorization approved, slot cancelled. The ordered queue is computed from those events by the current prioritization policy. That gives us a full audit trail (rebuild the queue as of any date), backtests for free (replaying events is the backtest), and lets any source feed the queue. The dataset already has this shape, and our feature script builds everything “as of” any date.

Physician confirmation

The engine proposes, a responsible physician confirms. Tier changes (up or down) wait for the patient's surgeon or the regulation physician. Small reorders inside a tier apply automatically because they come from the approved policy. Every confirmation and override is logged with who, when and why.

What makes it real software

  • Policies as versions. Weights and tier rules are approved by the clinical board (v1.0, v1.1). A new policy runs in shadow mode first (“with v1.2, these 14 patients would move”). Every ordering records which version produced it.
  • Integration through adapters. Tasy and MV talk HL7/FHIR; insurer authorizations use TISS (ANS); procedures and diagnoses use TUSS and CID-10. Mocks in the hackathon, but the adapter layer exists.
  • Multi-tenant. Network → hospital → specialty → queue, which matches fin-x selling to many hospitals.
  • LGPD and access control. Each role sees only what it needs; access and overrides are logged; patients consent before being contacted.
  • Safe failure. The engine is deterministic; if it goes down, everyone works from the last snapshot.

Matching patient, surgery, room and team

Filling a slot is a match, not “next in line”. Candidates come in priority order, filtered by readiness, procedure duration (duracao_media_min) fitting the slot, an ICU bed when necessita_uti = sim, and surgeon or specialty availability. The dataset has duration and ICU need per procedure but no capacity data, so demo capacity is assumed and we say so.

Prioritization engine

Rules decide the tier, the score orders patients inside a tier, and JEV is one input to the score. Readiness never lowers priority.

Step 0: clean the data

Done in data/build_features.py. Normalize CID, priority and municipality; merge 22 duplicate requests into the earliest one; remove 24 requests that left the queue; drop orphan and duplicated events; rebuild the oncologic, diabetic and worsening flags from evidence. Result: 895 active requests.

Layer 1: tiers from hard rules (SWALIS)

The golden rule holds by construction: every C* lands in A1 or A2, and every patient the verifier calls “sem urgência clínica” (237 of them) lands in C or D. Aging prevents starvation: passing a tier's max wait raises time pressure, and a patient far past it can be promoted one tier with physician confirmation.

Layer 2: score from 0 to 100 inside each tier

score = w1·severity + w2·deterioration + w3·time_pressure + w4·predicted_risk + w5·frailty
  • Severity: procedure risk (1–5), pain, functional limitation.
  • Deterioration: worsening events in 30/90/180 days, days since the last one.
  • Time pressure: days waited ÷ tier max wait, linear up to 1 and convex after, so overdue patients climb fast.
  • Predicted risk: JEV output.
  • Frailty: age, ASA, comorbidities.

Ties are broken by entry date. Note that 174 of 177 oncologic patients are already past 21 days, so time pressure alone cannot separate them; risk and deterioration have to.

Readiness, a separate dimension

pronto_para_agendar = pre-anesthetic evaluation done + valid pre-op exams + HbA1c ≤ 8.5. Only 136 of 895 are ready today. Readiness filters who can take a slot and never pushes a patient down.

The manual fields are unreliable

  • Manual priority: 90% never revised. 34 oncologic patients are baixa; 34 non-oncologic patients with no limitation are alta.
  • flag_oncologico disagrees with the CID in 44 rows (23 false yes, 21 false no).
  • flag_piora_clinica is não for 156 requests that have worsening events.
  • Average wait 389 days; 357 requests have waited more than 360 days, past SWALIS class D.

We use these only as the baseline to beat, never as model inputs.

JEV: the first risk score

One feature row per request, computed as of a date so we can backtest without leaking the future. The exact list is MODEL_FEATURES in the script (54 features).

Goes in

  • Patient: age, sex, ASA, comorbidity count and key comorbidities (DM2, ICC, ischemic heart disease, DPOC, DRC, anticoagulants, AF, obesity, smoking)
  • Diagnosis: oncologic by CID, uncertain CID, urgent CID, positive biopsy
  • Procedure: surgical risk, size, ICU need
  • Clinical status: pain, functional limitation (+ missing flags)
  • Events in 30/90/180 days: ER visits, hospitalizations, days hospitalized, imaging progression, re-evaluations, reported worsening, no-shows, days since last worsening, HbA1c and trend
  • Time: days in queue, days since last review
  • Text flags from the LLM, as booleans

Stays out

  • Name and IDs
  • Municipality and distance (equity; logistics only)
  • Manual priority and manual flags (noisy, human bias; baseline only)
  • Pre-op fields (readiness, not severity)
  • Raw free text

Label

The dataset has no outcomes, so we use a proxy: features as of the reference date minus the horizon, label = worsening event (ER visit, hospitalization, imaging progression, re-evaluation worse, reported worsening) within the horizon.

After cleaning, out/train_h90.csv has 734 rows and 158 positives (22%), with no empty values. The label tracks the obvious signals: 72% positive with a worsening in the previous 90 days vs 18% without; 52% for oncologic vs 17%. Enough for a regularized logistic regression with about 20 features. Caveat for the pitch: worsening events cluster near the reference date in this synthetic data, so the model leans on static features.

LLMs and agents

The LLM extracts and explains. The engine decides, deterministically.

  1. Justification text → structured signals. JSON with alarm category and quoted evidence. The verifier's regex catches 124 alarm mentions but not negation (“sem sinais de encarceramento”) or paraphrase. Our script uses regex stand-ins (txt_*, obs_*) with the same column names, so swapping in the LLM changes nothing downstream.
  2. Observações → signals. Exits, reported worsening, the 12 “Familiar informa internação recente na UPA” notes (worsening with no event), unreachable patients, transport / TFD needs.
  3. Consistency checks. Flags vs CID and events, positive biopsy with benign CID, vague justifications.
  4. Explanations. Plain-language receipts and “why is A ahead of B?”.
  5. Agents simulating the medical team (surgeon, anesthesiologist, regulator), only on borderline cases: near a tier boundary or with a wide Monte Carlo range.

Explainability

A receipt for every position, like reason codes on a credit score. The score is a sum of parts, so the receipt is exact.

Position 12 · Maria, 64 · Colecistectomia
Tier B  (upgraded: ER visit on 03/10)
Score 78
  +30  severity
  +22  deterioration  (2 events in 30 days)
  +18  time           (91% of max wait used)
   +8  predicted risk (P = 0.34)
Ready to schedule: no (pre-anesthetic pending)
  • The deliverable's justificativa comes from a deterministic template on all ~895 rows, e.g. “A2 (neoplasia maligna C50.9); 3 meses na fila (prazo 21 dias); PS em 10/2026; risco 0.41”. Reproducible, cheap, always consistent with the order. The LLM writes longer explanations on demand.
  • Physician confirmation. Tier changes wait for a responsible physician. Overrides need a written reason. Everything is logged and shown on the receipt.
  • Alerts. Sudden deterioration (tier jump) to surgeon and nurse; about to pass max wait to regulation; suspected malignancy with benign CID for CID review.

Robustness

How we show the order is trustworthy, not just a formula we picked.

  • Official verifier first. Format, coverage, golden rule, plus indicators to quote: median position of oncologic patients, share of recent ER/hospitalization in the first half.
  • Monte Carlo on the weights. Sample weight sets around the defaults, recompute the order, and report each patient's range: “between positions 8 and 14 in 95% of scenarios”. Wide range goes to a human and to the agent review.
  • Backtest without leakage. Features as of a past date, then check whether the top-ranked really worsened after it (precision@k, AUC), against the current method.
  • Queue simulation. Assumed OR capacity. Compare FIFO + manual priority vs Pulso on patients past max wait, worsening while waiting, wait of A1/A2 and of C/D (we do not starve anyone).
  • Sensitivity. Remove one score part at a time and measure the change (Kendall tau).
  • Equity. Positions by age group, sex, municipality and distance. Age is used as frailty, and we say so.

Frontend

One view per hospital role, all reading the same queue. Pages read from the API only; no prioritization logic in the frontend.

Shared pieces: patient card (receipt, event timeline, readiness checklist, Monte Carlo range, LLM explanation, confirmation history), tier and readiness badges, movement arrows, alert badges, and an “as of date” selector on every view.

Demo flow

  1. Queue with the current method: one patient stuck low despite getting worse.
  2. Replay: an ER visit arrives, Pulso proposes moving the patient up.
  3. Surgeon view: the surgeon confirms, with a reason.
  4. Patient card: open the receipt, ask “why is A ahead of B?”.
  5. Nurse view: the patient appears on today's contacts; the WhatsApp check-in reports worsening.
  6. Fill a slot: the top ready candidates appear.
  7. Policies: move a weight slider, show the robustness range and shadow mode.
  8. End on the before/after numbers.
